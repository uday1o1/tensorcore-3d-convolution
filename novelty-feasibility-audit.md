# Novelty and Feasibility Review: Windowed Convolution in Three Dimensions

This review follows two adversarial passes, novelty and feasibility, searching actively for reasons the idea fails or already exists rather than reasons it works, and it rests on a direct reproduction of Im2win's published 2D comparison.

## Summary

Is this novel? Yes. Reproducing the seed technique's own benchmark on its own GPU class, rather than assuming its headline number, shows that the kernel result reproduces on the first RTX 3090 host (37.1 TFLOPS against cuDNN's 23.8 on conv9, a ratio of 1.56, against the 1.4 overall improvement the paper reports across its twelve layers; cuDNN's own throughput on that layer varies between runs from 17.6 to 42.4 TFLOPS, so the ratio depends on which cuDNN run it is set against, and on a second RTX 3090 with the same driver and libraries cuDNN ran at 41.2 to 41.4 TFLOPS in every one of 25 fresh processes against the kernel's 36.3 to 36.9, so there cuDNN is faster at the kernel level), but the published timing covers the convolution kernel alone. The `image2window` transform that produces the kernel's input runs on the host outside the timing loop, while cuDNN's implicit GEMM performs the equivalent work inside its timed kernel. Implementing that transform on the GPU, which a usable integration requires since the released one is host-side, and measuring it directly moves the end-to-end ratio to 1.13 on the first host, close to parity, and to 0.65 on the second. The contribution is therefore not a speedup claim. It is a reproduction with complete accounting, a mechanistic account of what decides the trade (arithmetic intensity, the compute performed per byte of materialized buffer), and a falsifiable prediction that follows from it: because reduction depth grows from `C x 9` to `C x 27` with the added spatial axis, three-dimensional convolution should be more favorable to windowed methods than two-dimensional convolution, not less. That runs opposite to the natural intuition about compounding overlap redundancy, and it is what the 3D extension is built to test.

## Novelty Audit: Survives with Named Limitations

**Why it survives.** Three separable claims, each checked.

First, the 2D-to-3D extension of this windowing scheme is unclaimed. Four papers spanning 2022 to 2026 were read in full text and cover only two-dimensional convolution; no transposed, grouped, depthwise, or volumetric variant appears anywhere across them.

Second, the accounting correction is unclaimed and verified by direct measurement rather than argument. We found no published source stating that Im2win's advantage is consumed by materialization cost.

Third, a dedicated search for competing work on the new target found none. The nearest result is NVIDIA's own optimized nnU-Net for PyTorch, which uses standard automatic mixed precision and TF32 on ordinary `Conv3d` calls: that is the cuDNN baseline this project measures against, not a competing windowed implementation. A second search for windowed convolution or Im2win in medical image segmentation returned no three-dimensional or segmentation-specific application. The search methodology can find real competing work: it surfaced an April 2026 depthwise-convolution CUDA optimization paper (source 1 of the literature survey).

**Named limitation 1.** This is a narrow, single-lineage examination of one technique family, not a general claim about GPU convolution, and should be presented that way.

**Named limitation 2.** The 1.13 figure uses a GPU transform written for this project rather than the authors' own, because the released transform is host-side OpenMP and no device version exists to instrument. The measured expansion of 2.89 times matches what the released code's indexing implies, and the transform achieved 713 GB/s, so the implementation is a reasonable stand-in rather than a strawman. A hand-tuned transform could be somewhat faster, which would move the ratio up modestly; materializing the buffer at all is intrinsic to the method and cannot be engineered away. One residual imprecision is disclosed: the 1.13 figure combines a best-of-50 kernel time with a mean-of-30 transform time, which is small relative to the gap.

**Result.** The prediction was tested and agrees in direction, on different layer sets in two and three dimensions: a shape-generic windowed convolution, verified correct against cuDNN on shapes the released kernels cannot run, gives a geometric mean ratio of 0.591 over nnU-Net's ten 2D layers and 0.654 over nine of its 3D layers (the 4-cubed bottleneck layer excluded) at matched materialization, with 0.429 for the higher-materialization three-dimensional variant. All configurations lose to cuDNN, by geometric-mean factors of roughly 1.5 to 1.7 at matched materialization and 2.3 with k^2 materialization, three times as much at kernel size 3, and the published figures come from hand-tuned kernels, which on conv9 are 2.9 times faster than a generic implementation of the same algorithm.

**Named limitation 3.** The central prediction was stated directionally and without attached numbers, deliberately. No modelled per-layer ratios are given, because a model would have to assume the windowed kernel sustains its benchmark throughput at nnU-Net's layer shapes, and the output checks show the released conv9 kernel producing no output at the nnU-Net 2D shape checked, at nnU-Net's 2D batch size of 16 and at its base width of 30 channels. There is therefore no measured basis for any per-layer figure, and producing one is the object of the implementation work rather than an input to it. The structural argument, that reduction depth grows with the added spatial axis and amortizes materialization, stands on its own as reasoning and is what the experiment tests.

**Named limitation 4.** This research area changes on a timescale of weeks. A search for closer work is repeated before publication.

**Named limitation 5.** The choice of nnU-Net as the substitution target follows from a compute-share argument (99.6 percent of its FLOPs are in convolution, transposed convolution included, measured with fvcore) rather than from architectural interest. nnU-Net's plain 3D U-Net is an older, less parameter-efficient design than MedNeXt. The contribution is about convolution implementation, not segmentation architecture, and should be presented that way.

**Named limitation 6.** A two-dimensional configuration of the same network is a legitimate alternative that the existing kernel already supports, which would make the 3D work practically unnecessary if 2D were competitive on accuracy for this task. Rather than argue the point, the 2D arm is included as the control condition and the comparison is measured directly.

## Feasibility Audit: Survives with Named Limitations

Based on verified resource facts and direct measurement, not duration estimates.

**Code and technique access.** The Im2win codebase is real, substantive, Apache-2.0 licensed, and verified to build and run on this project's exact environment (CUDA 12.8, sm_86, RTX 3090), reproducing the authors' kernel-level result at 37.1 TFLOPS. Its repository contains only forward-pass CUDA kernels, with no backward pass and no PyTorch or LibTorch operator binding anywhere; the `libtorch_tflops` directory links LibTorch only as a comparison target. The Tensor Core kernels are hardcoded to half precision; fp32 exists only in the separate CUDA-core kernels, which are likewise forward-only; unlike the Tensor Core kernels, their launch grids are computed from the problem shape.

More seriously, direct testing with output verification established that the kernels are specialized to individual benchmark layers rather than general. Launch geometry is literal: conv9 uses `dim3(CEIL_DIV(M, TLIE_M_PER_BLOCK), 18, 54 * 6)` where 54 is its own output height, conv4 uses `dim3(CEIL_DIV(M, TLIE_M_PER_BLOCK), 109, 109 * 2)` where 109 is its output height. Changing batch size from the benchmark's 256 to 128, 16, or 4 yields entirely zero output; changing channels to 30 does the same; changing spatial extent to 128 yields 18 percent populated output. None of these raise an error. This means there is no existing general implementation to extend, and that any timing measured without verifying the output is meaningless. Both facts shape the implementation: it is construction of a general kernel rather than a port, and correctness verification precedes every reported timing.

**Baseline access.** nnU-Net's v1-lineage codebase, bundled inside MedNeXt's repository, is directly usable and was verified to build, run forward and backward passes, and profile correctly on the project's GPU. MedNeXt's repository is complete and usable; its most recent commit is dated 2 November 2024, and the later MedNeXt-v2 is distributed through the nnU-Net repository instead. EffiDec3D's repository is official and recent; its UT Austin Research License permits academic, research, experimental and personal use and permits modification subject to Section 3.2, which requires derivative works to be provided to the licensor under the same terms; Section 3.1(a) prohibits redistribution, so its code is cloned by the user rather than vendored into this repository.

**Data access.** The Medical Segmentation Decathlon is fully public with no registration or approval process. Verified directly: 131 labeled training volumes and 70 unlabeled test volumes for the Liver task, with three classes; the 131 training volumes were downloaded, converted, and preprocessed to completion on the project's GPU instance.

**Compute access.** Verified by measurement on the project's rented RTX 3090, at the planner's actual configuration (`base_num_features` of 30, 128 cubed patch, batch size 2): nnU-Net's 3D U-Net runs at 338 ms per training iteration with mixed precision and 733 ms without, using 4.79 GB and 17.10 GB peak respectively, and 59.2 ms per patch at inference under fp16 autocast (53.4 ms with cuDNN autotuning enabled, in the whole-network substitution run). The network is 29.70M parameters and 422.1 G multiply-accumulates (fvcore's FLOP count) per 128-cubed patch. The GPU class matches the one used in Im2win's own published evaluation, which makes the reproduction directly comparable.

**Named limitation 1.** The windowing scheme has never been exercised on a genuinely three-dimensional kernel at any size. The open question is dimensionality rather than kernel size.

**Named limitation 2.** nnU-Net's 3D U-Net has a base width of 30 and convolution channel widths up to 640 (verified directly), across spatial extents from 128 down to 4, at batch size 2. The released kernels are two-dimensional, and the conv9 kernel returns zero or partial output at each nnU-Net value tested (batch 16, 30 channels, spatial extents 32 and 128). A shape-generic design is therefore a requirement rather than a refinement, and it is the substantial part of the implementation work.

**Named limitation 3.** Profiling establishes a hard ceiling on any end-to-end claim. Convolution accounts for 65.6 percent of GPU kernel time in inference, so by Amdahl's law even an infinitely fast convolution kernel caps end-to-end inference improvement at about 2.91 times. A kernel-level result must not be presented as if it transferred directly to end-to-end performance.

**Named limitation 4.** The backward kernels, if pursued, have no existing reference to validate against beyond gradient-checking against autograd on small synthetic inputs, and the weight-gradient pass has a different GEMM shape from the forward pass rather than mirroring it. This is the highest-risk element in the project and is therefore scoped as a secondary objective, outside the critical path for the primary inference result.

**Named limitation 5.** The fp16-only kernels run inside autocast, where nnU-Net's convolutions already take fp16 inputs (InstanceNorm is not on autocast's float32 list); any part of the network run in fp32 would need explicit casts at every convolution boundary, an overhead absent from the isolated kernel benchmark, so the two numbers must be reported separately rather than conflated.
